# Rejected Lessons

Approaches the AI should avoid, with reasons. Each entry records the proposed lesson, why it was rejected, and what to do instead.

## Rejection 1: Don't signal tech with tech aesthetics

**Approach to avoid:** Adding geometric or technical visual elements (circuit patterns, precise lines, mono-spaced type, data motifs) to an organic design just to signal that the product involves technology.

**Why rejected:** The AI's instinct to "show the tech" produced designs that prioritized capability signaling over visual interest. The human chose the option that led with warmth and surprise. Technical capability is better communicated through secondary elements (UI accent colors, data-specific typography) or through the product experience itself — not by overriding the logo's core concept.

**What to do instead:** When the product involves technology but the audience values community and organic qualities, let the tech be invisible in the brand identity. Lead with what makes someone stop and look. Push technical signifiers to interaction-layer elements where they serve a functional purpose.
