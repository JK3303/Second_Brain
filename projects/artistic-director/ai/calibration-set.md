# Calibration Set

A small, job-neutral test set for calibrating AI-human collaboration. Each exercise uses public or self-created material only. No client material in Phase 1.

## Exercise format

Each calibration exercise should include:

1. **Brief** — what was asked.
2. **AI output** — what the AI generated.
3. **Human critique** — specific feedback on the output.
4. **Revised output** — the AI's revision after critique.
5. **Accepted lesson** — what reusable rule emerged.
6. **Rejected lesson** — what approach should be avoided, with reasons.
7. **Remaining gap** — what the AI still misunderstood.

---

## Exercise 1: Rootmind Visual Identity

**Brief:** Design a visual identity direction (name, logo concept, color palette, typography feel, and one-line positioning statement) for a fictional AI-powered community garden management platform called "Rootmind." The platform helps neighborhood gardens coordinate planting schedules, share harvests, and track soil health using sensor data. The audience is urban gardeners aged 25-55 who value sustainability and local community.

**AI output:** Three materially different directions:

- **Option A — "Living Circuit":** Circuit board pattern forming a root system. Tech-forward palette (deep brown, electric green). Mono-spaced type. Positioning: "Your garden's intelligence, rooted in community."
- **Option B — "Generous Earth":** Hand-drawn imperfect circle containing two overlapping leaves that form a brain silhouette in negative space. Warm palette (terracotta, sage, cream). Rounded serif + friendly sans-serif. Positioning: "Grow together, harvest together."
- **Option C — "Data Garden":** Minimal wordmark with data-point replacing the dot of the "i." Sophisticated palette (forest teal, warm gold). Single geometric sans-serif family. Positioning: "See what your soil is telling you."

**Human critique:** Option B was the most interesting to viewers. The leaf-brain negative space logo was the core concept and must be protected — nothing should obscure that dual read.

**Revised output (revision 1):** Added a geometric bisecting line to the logo and a teal accent color for data elements. Shifted the brain negative space to "optional."

**Human critique of revision 1:** The leaf-brain negative space is the concept, not optional. The geometric line competes with it. Don't add elements that obscure the discovery moment.

**Revised output (revision 2):** Dropped the bisecting line entirely. Adjusted leaf overlap so inner edges follow brain hemisphere contours more deliberately while still reading as natural leaves first. The brain reads in cream negative space against sage/terracotta leaves. Teal accent moved to UI elements only, never in the logo. Logo has one job: leaves first, brain second, surprise in between.

**Accepted lesson:** When a logo concept has a deliberate reveal (a secondary image in negative space, a hidden meaning), protect that reveal at all costs. Don't add competing visual elements that split the viewer's attention or obscure the discovery moment. The surprise is the concept — diluting it dilutes everything.

**Rejected lesson:** Don't add geometric or technical elements to an organic design just to "signal" a capability (like AI). If the brand tension needs to exist, push it to secondary elements (accent colors, data-specific typography) rather than into the logo itself.

**Remaining gap:** The AI's first instinct when told "this is an AI platform" was to add visible tech signifiers (circuit patterns, geometric lines, mono-spaced type). Two of three initial options led with technology. The human valued visual interest and viewer engagement over technical signaling. The AI may still default to "show the tech" when the better move is to let the tech be invisible and lead with what makes someone stop and look.
