# Evaluation Rubric — Rebranding Proposals

Nine criteria, scored 1–5. Every shortlisted proposal is scored on all nine by the AD.

## How to use it

- **The AD scores.** AI may pre-populate evidence and flag inconsistencies; it does not assign scores.
- **Score each criterion across all proposals before moving to the next.** Scoring one proposal fully, then the next, produces halo effects.
- **The total is a discussion aid, not a verdict.** A proposal may win on total and still be the wrong recommendation — say so explicitly if that happens.
- **Anchors are defined at 1, 3, and 5.** Use 2 and 4 for intermediate cases.
- **Every score of 1 or 2 requires a written reason.** Every score of 5 requires a written reason.

Criteria 1–5 measure the creative proposition. Criteria 6–7 measure the integrity of the evidence behind it. Criteria 8–9 measure whether it can actually be decided on and carried forward. A proposal that is strong on 1–5 and weak on 6–7 is not ready for review, whatever its creative merit.

---

## 1. Strategic distinctiveness

Does this occupy a genuinely different strategic position from its siblings and from the observable competitive field?

- **1** — A restyling of another proposal. Differs in palette, type, or tagline only.
- **3** — Genuinely different center of gravity, but the customer promise overlaps substantially with a sibling.
- **5** — Different center of gravity, different customer promise, different proof mechanism. A customer could articulate the difference unprompted.

## 2. Brand authenticity

Is this credible for *this* organization, given what is actually known about it?

- **1** — Requires the organization to be something no evidence supports.
- **3** — Plausible, but leans on one or more unverified assumptions about the organization.
- **5** — Grounded in registered observations. Extends what the organization demonstrably already is.

*Guard:* a territory that is exciting but requires invented history, invented capability, or invented values scores low here regardless of its appeal.

## 3. Customer relevance

Does this speak to the people who actually buy, in terms that matter to them?

- **1** — Addresses a customer the evidence does not show exists.
- **3** — Relevant to a plausible customer, but the customer definition is inferred rather than evidenced.
- **5** — Anchored in registered evidence about actual customers, and the promise answers something they demonstrably care about.

*Guard:* if customer evidence is missing, no proposal may score above 3 here. Say so rather than scoring on intuition.

## 4. Visual coherence

Do the logo or naming treatment, palette, typography, imagery, materials, and graphic language form one system?

- **1** — Elements contradict each other, or are assembled from unrelated references.
- **3** — Coherent, but at least one element is doing no work or could belong to any of the proposals.
- **5** — Every element reinforces the premise. Removing one would visibly weaken the whole.

## 5. Verbal coherence

Do the positioning, customer promise, naming, and sample messaging speak in one voice that matches the visual system?

- **1** — The writing contradicts the visual identity, or is generic category language.
- **3** — Consistent voice, but the sample messaging could sit under a sibling proposal without friction.
- **5** — The voice is inseparable from the premise. Sample messaging could not be transplanted to another territory.

## 6. Evidence discipline

Are observations, interpretations, hypotheses, human decisions, and unresolved claims kept distinct?

- **1** — Claims are blended into a single confident voice. Assumptions are presented as facts.
- **3** — Mostly separated, but some interpretations read as observations.
- **5** — Every claim carries its type. Unknowns are listed with owners rather than filled in. Nothing invented.

*Guard:* this criterion is non-negotiable at the package level. Any proposal scoring 1 or 2 is returned to Step 6, not presented.

## 7. Rights clarity

Is the provenance and rights status of every asset and named element known and labeled?

- **1** — Unlabeled assets, or assets whose origin cannot be reconstructed.
- **3** — All assets labeled, but rights status for one or more is unresolved and the resolution path is unclear.
- **5** — Every asset labeled `sourced`, `generated`, `placeholder`, `self-created`, or `rights-cleared`; every unresolved item has a named owner and a resolution path. Names and taglines are flagged for clearance before any use.

*Guard:* "no rights issue identified" is not the same as "rights cleared." Only clearance by an authorized party produces `rights-cleared`.

## 8. Decision readiness

Could the decision-maker act on this today, with what is in front of them?

- **1** — Key material is missing, placeholder-heavy, or the proposal cannot be understood without the AD narrating it.
- **3** — Understandable and complete, but a material open question would surface immediately on reading.
- **5** — Self-contained. Strengths, risks, assumptions, uncertainties, and implications are all stated. The decision-maker has what they need to select, combine, hold, or reject.

## 9. Practical extensibility

If chosen, does this direction keep working — across applications, over time, and without the AD present?

- **1** — Works only in the shown applications. Breaks at other sizes, media, or use cases.
- **3** — Extends reasonably, but depends on a content engine or capability the organization may not have.
- **5** — Extends predictably to unshown applications; a third party could apply it correctly from the documentation; the content it requires is producible with evidenced capability.

*Guard:* score the organization's demonstrated capability, not its aspirational capability.
