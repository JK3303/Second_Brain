## What was produced

Two visual concepts rendered inline:

### 1. Brand moodboard
- **Color palette:** Warm cream (#F5F0EB), soft gold (#C9A96E), warm charcoal (#3D3530)
- **Gemstone accent colors:** Emerald, ruby, teal, sapphire, opal fire
- **Typography direction:** Humanist serif for headlines/wordmark, clean sans for body
- **Tone cards:** Warm not precious, specific not superlative, collaborative not transactional, occasion-aware not occasion-dependent
- **Voice examples:** Do/don't pairs showing the brand voice in practice
- **Wordmark concept:** "Grace Gems" in serif on dark warm background, with sample hero treatment

### 2. Homepage wireframe
- **Navigation:** Stories, Collections, Custom, About, Contact (no "Shop" link)
- **Hero:** Single customer story with on-person photography and quote
- **Occasion navigation:** "What moment are you marking?" with four tiles: Engagement, Anniversary, Milestone, Self-gift
- **Featured collection:** Three products from one stone family with on-person photography
- **Customer voices:** Two review excerpts with piece details
- **Custom invitation:** Warm CTA — "Tell us what you're imagining"
- **Footer:** Secondary nav, location

## Key design decisions reflected

1. Maker is anonymous — no individual named anywhere
2. Voice is "we" — "We'll design a piece that's yours alone"
3. Story-first — the hero is a customer narrative, not a product grid
4. The entire site is shoppable — no separate "shop" section
5. Warm cream/gold/charcoal palette avoids both clinical white and luxury black-and-gold
6. Photography placeholders indicate on-person style, not floating-on-white

## Open for AD review

- Does the color palette feel right? Too warm? Too muted?
- Is the serif wordmark direction correct, or should we explore other type treatments?
- Is the homepage structure right? Too much before products? Not enough story?
- Does the occasion-based navigation feel natural or forced?
