# Website Experience — "Your Stone, Your Story"

**Relay ID:** GG-AD-P2-001
**Territory:** C — Customer-centric
**Date:** 2026-08-05
**Status:** Draft — internal exploration only. Not approved for build, deployment, or publication.

---

## Design Philosophy

The website is not a catalog with a story bolted on. It is a story-first experience where every path eventually leads to a piece of jewelry — but the journey matters as much as the destination.

The central design challenge: move from Etsy's 1,597-item grid to a curated, narrative experience without losing the product breadth that drives revenue. The solution is layered navigation — story on the surface, depth underneath.

---

## Site Structure

### Primary Navigation

```
Grace Gems
├── Stories          → Customer narratives, the emotional entry point
├── Collections      → Stone-family browsing (emerald, ruby, opal, etc.)
├── Made for You     → The personalized jewelry journey
├── About            → The workshop, sourcing, our craft
└── Contact          → Direct line to the Grace Gems team
```

**No "Shop" link in primary nav.** Every section contains shoppable pieces. The entire site is the shop — but it never feels like one until the moment you're ready.

### Secondary Navigation (footer or utility)

```
├── All Pieces       → Full catalog with filters (the Etsy-style browse for returning customers)
├── Care & Warranty  → Jewelry care, repair warranty, returns
├── FAQ              → Shipping, customs, layaway, sizing
├── Gemstone Guide   → Educational reference (supports Territory B's stone knowledge as a resource)
└── Reviews          → Imported from Etsy + new on-site reviews
```

## Page Concepts

### Homepage

**Purpose:** Establish emotional tone. Show that this is personal jewelry, not a catalog.

**Structure (top to bottom):**

1. **Hero — a single customer story.** One piece, one person, one moment. Full-width image of the piece being worn. A two-sentence story overlay. Rotates weekly or monthly — not a carousel. One story at a time.

2. **"What moment are you marking?"** A gentle prompt with 4–5 occasion tiles: Engagement, Anniversary, Milestone, Self-Gift, Something Custom. Each links to a curated selection or the custom page. This replaces traditional category navigation with intent-based entry.

3. **Featured collection.** 3–4 pieces from a single stone family, shown in the on-person photography style. Brief context about the stone. Links to the full collection.

4. **"From our customers."** 2–3 short review excerpts with the piece they purchased. Real names (with permission), real occasions.

5. **The custom invitation.** A warm, short paragraph about the custom process with a single CTA: "Tell us what you're imagining." Voice is "we" — the Grace Gems team, no individual named.

6. **Footer.** Secondary nav, contact, social links, newsletter signup.

**What the homepage is NOT:**
- A product grid
- A slideshow of promotions
- A wall of text about the brand

---

### Stories Page

**Purpose:** The brand's primary content vehicle. Real pieces, real people, real meaning.

**Format:** A scrolling feed of customer stories — each one a short narrative (150–300 words) with:
- The piece (product photo + on-person photo)
- The person (first name, general location — "Mary in Denver")
- The moment (what the piece was for, what it means)
- The stone (brief material note — "a natural Australian black opal")

**Sourcing stories:** Start by asking existing customers (from Etsy reviews) if they'd share their story. The existing customer relationships make this possible.

**Interaction:** Each story links to the piece (or similar available pieces) and to the custom page. "Want something like this? Let's talk."

**Cadence:** One new story per month is sustainable. Quality over quantity.

---

### Collection Pages (by stone family)

**Purpose:** Let the stones lead the browsing experience.

**Structure:**

1. **Stone introduction.** A short, poetic-but-factual paragraph about the stone: what makes it distinctive, where Grace Gems sources it, what to look for. Not a geology lecture — a jeweler's appreciation.

2. **Featured piece.** One hero piece from the collection in on-person photography. Why this stone was chosen for this design.

3. **Product grid.** The full collection in that stone family, with filters for type (ring, earring, necklace, bracelet), metal (14k, 18k, rose/white/yellow gold), and price range.

4. **Stone details panel.** Expandable: origin, hardness, care instructions, what "natural and untreated" means for this stone. Educational but optional — it's there for the curious, not forced on everyone.

**Product cards show:**
- On-person or stone-portrait photography (not floating-on-white)
- Stone type and carat weight
- Metal and gold purity
- Sale price and original price
- "Custom available" badge where applicable

---

### Individual Product Page

**Purpose:** Give the customer everything they need to decide — and a human path if they're not sure.

**Structure:**

1. **Image gallery.** Lead with on-person photography. Include stone-portrait macro. Include the standard product shots for detail. 4–6 images.

2. **Story hook.** If this piece has a customer story associated with it: a one-line teaser linking to the full story. "Mary wore this to her daughter's graduation."

3. **Details.**
   - Stone: type, origin, carat weight, cut, treatment status
   - Metal: type, purity, weight
   - Dimensions
   - Price (sale and original)

4. **Add to cart + customization options.**
   - Ring size selector
   - Metal preference (if variants exist)
   - "Want this in a different stone or setting? Let's talk." — direct link to custom inquiry

5. **Care and warranty.** Expandable. Free shipping, 30-day returns, 1-year repair warranty.

6. **"You might also love."** 3–4 related pieces from the same stone family or occasion category.

---

### Made for You Page

**Purpose:** Make the "made for you" process inviting, not intimidating. This is a conversation, not a form.

**Structure:**

1. **Opening.** A short paragraph about what custom work means to Grace Gems, how the process works, what to expect. Voice is "we."

2. **How it works.** Three steps, simply stated:
   - **Tell us.** What's the occasion? Do you have a stone or style in mind? Share as much or as little as you want.
   - **We design.** Our team sketches options, sources the stone, and sends photos and renders for your approval.
   - **We craft.** Your piece is handmade in 2–3 weeks. You receive it with a complimentary jewelry box and optional appraisal certificate.

3. **Custom stories.** 2–3 examples of past custom work — the brief, the process, the result. Shows the range of what's possible.

4. **The inquiry.** A simple, warm form:
   - Your name
   - What's the occasion? (optional — dropdown or free text)
   - What are you imagining? (free text — generous text area)
   - Budget range (optional — dropdown with ranges)
   - Email
   - Attach an inspiration image (optional)

   Submit button: "Start the conversation"

**What this page is NOT:**
- A clinical order form with SKU fields
- A chatbot
- A page that makes the customer feel like they need to know exactly what they want before reaching out

---

### About Page — "The Workshop"

**Purpose:** Build trust through transparency. Show the craft without centering an individual.

**Structure:**

1. **Who we are.** A brief introduction to Grace Gems as a team — how long we've been crafting, what drives the work. No individual is named or photographed. The skill speaks through the pieces, not a personal brand.

2. **The process.** How a piece goes from idea to finished jewelry. Photography of the workshop, the tools, the stages — hands and work, not faces.

3. **Sourcing.** Where the stones come from. Name the countries, describe the relationships. "Natural and untreated" explained — what it means and why it matters.

4. **The promise.** The shop policies restated as commitments, not a numbered list:
   - Every stone is natural and untreated
   - Every piece is handmade in solid gold
   - Free worldwide shipping
   - 30-day returns, 1-year repair warranty
   - Appraisal certificates available

## Responsive Considerations

- Mobile-first design — most jewelry browsing happens on phones
- Product photography must be optimized for small screens (crop tighter on mobile)
- Story content flows naturally in single-column on mobile
- Custom inquiry form must be easy to complete on a phone
- Touch-friendly image galleries (swipe, not click-through)
