## Launch Narrative

Grace Gems isn't launching a new brand. It's revealing the one that already exists.

For six years, Grace Gems has been making custom fine jewelry for people who care about what their pieces mean — not just what they look like. Over 2,400 customers already know this. The independent website and brand upgrade give that reality a proper home.

The launch is not "come see our new website." It is: "We've been doing this for years. Now you can see the full picture."

---

## Campaign Concept: "What's Your Stone?"

### Core Idea

Every person who owns a Grace Gems piece has a reason they chose the stone they chose. The campaign asks that question — and lets real answers be the content.

"What's your stone?" works on two levels:
1. Literal: what gemstone are you drawn to, and why?
2. Emotional: what moment, memory, or meaning does your piece hold?

### Format

Short-form customer portraits. Each one follows the same simple structure:

```
[Customer first name]
[The stone]
[The piece]
[The story — 2–3 sentences, in their words]
```

Example (fictional — for structure illustration only):

```
Mary
Australian Black Opal
Custom dangle earrings with sapphires and rubies
"My mother collected opals her whole life. When she passed,
I wanted something that carried her love of color.
Grace Gems made these from a stone she would have picked herself."
```

These portraits appear across channels: website Stories page, social media, email, and any advertising.

## Content Strategy

### Three content pillars

**1. Customer Stories** (primary — 60% of content)
- The "What's Your Stone?" portraits
- Longer narrative features for the website Stories page
- Review highlights from Etsy, repackaged with context and permission

**2. Stone Knowledge** (supporting — 25% of content)
- Short educational pieces about individual gemstone families
- "Did you know?" format for social: one surprising fact per stone
- Sourcing notes: where this stone came from, what makes it special
- This pillar borrows Territory B's strength without letting it take over

**3. Process Glimpses** (supporting — 15% of content)
- Workshop moments: a stone being set, a wax model, a finished piece before shipping
- The designer's perspective: why this stone was chosen for this design (voice is "we," not a named individual)
- Behind the custom process: from inquiry to finished piece
- This pillar borrows Territory A's strength as a trust-builder

### Content cadence (suggested — scalable up or down)

| Channel | Frequency | Primary Pillar |
|---------|-----------|---------------|
| Website Stories | 1 new story/month | Customer Stories |
| Instagram/social | 2–3 posts/week | Mix of all three |
| Email newsletter | 1–2/month | Customer Stories + featured pieces |
| Gemstone Guide (website) | Evergreen — build over time | Stone Knowledge |

## Channel Approach

### Website (primary)

The website is the campaign's permanent home. Every piece of campaign content lives there first, then distributes to other channels. The Stories page accumulates over time into the brand's most valuable asset — a growing library of proof that this jewelry means something to real people.

### Instagram (primary social)

Jewelry is visual. Instagram is where the on-person photography and customer stories get daily visibility.

**Post types:**
- Customer story portrait (carousel: piece photo + story text)
- Stone close-up with one-line educational caption
- Process video/photo (15–30 second workshop moments)
- New piece reveal in on-person photography

**Avoid:**
- Generic inspirational quotes over jewelry photos
- Hard promotional posts ("25% off everything!")
- Stock or templated graphics
- Reposting Etsy listings as-is

### Email

Direct channel to existing customers and new subscribers. The most effective channel for repeat purchases and custom inquiries.

**Newsletter format:**
- One customer story (the anchor)
- 2–3 new or featured pieces
- One stone knowledge note
- CTA to custom page or featured collection

**Tone:** Personal, from the Grace Gems team. Not a corporate newsletter. No individual named as author.

### Etsy (transitional)

Etsy remains a sales channel and discovery engine. The brand upgrade doesn't mean abandoning it — it means the independent site becomes the primary experience, and Etsy becomes one distribution channel among several.

**Etsy-specific actions:**
- Update shop banner and profile to reflect the new brand identity
- Add "Visit gracegems.com for the full experience" to the shop announcement (within Etsy's policies)
- Maintain listings and customer service on Etsy as normal
- Use Etsy as a lead-generation channel — customers discover on Etsy, deepen the relationship on the website

## Launch Sequence (suggested phasing)

### Pre-launch (before website goes live)

1. Reach out to 5–10 past customers for "What's Your Stone?" stories
2. Commission or produce on-person photography for 20–30 key pieces
3. Finalize brand identity (wordmark, colors, typography)
4. Build the website
5. Set up email capture / newsletter tool

### Launch

1. Website goes live — announce to existing Etsy customers via Etsy message and email
2. First 3–5 customer stories published on Stories page
3. Instagram transitions to new brand identity and content style
4. Launch email to subscriber list

### Post-launch (ongoing)

1. One new customer story per month
2. Consistent social posting (2–3/week)
3. Monthly newsletter
4. Gradual build-out of Gemstone Guide content
5. Monitor which content drives custom inquiries and adjust

## Success Indicators (internal only — not metrics commitments)

These are creative signals, not business KPIs. Business metrics are Robert's domain.

- Customer stories generate custom inquiries ("I want something like what Mary has")
- Returning Etsy customers find and use the independent site
- The brand voice feels consistent across website, email, and social
- Product photography in the new style feels distinctly Grace Gems, not generic jewelry
- The custom page generates inquiries from people who describe what they want in emotional terms, not just specifications

## Risks

1. **Customer story dependency.** If past customers don't participate, the campaign's primary content pillar is weak. Mitigation: start outreach early; even 3 stories is enough to launch.
2. **Photography investment.** The current Etsy photos (likely white-background product shots) won't carry the new brand. New photography is not optional — it's the most important single investment.
3. **Etsy-to-website transition friction.** Customers who know Grace Gems through Etsy may resist switching. The website must be obviously better — not just different.
4. **Content production sustainability.** 2–3 social posts per week + monthly stories + newsletter requires consistent effort. Who makes the content while the team makes jewelry?
5. **Brand voice consistency.** Moving from SEO-stuffed Etsy titles to a warm, personal voice is a real shift. All customer-facing writing needs to sound like the same brand.
