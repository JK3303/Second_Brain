# Bookkeeping basis and obligations

Before proposing entries, read the record's instructions, relevant formulas,
and source evidence. Determine whether it measures company cash movements,
accounting recognition, or unpaid obligations. Preserve that basis; do not
silently convert a cash tracker into accounting books. Ask only when missing
treatment materially changes an entry. A clarification supplies evidence, not
unrelated write or payment authority.

## Starting position and cutover

When adopting or refreshing an existing record, establish a dated starting position
before importing history. Identify account balances, pending processor funds,
restricted/third-party funds, outstanding invoices, payables and reimbursements.
Keep each opening obligation linked to its original evidence; do not manufacture
a new current-period purchase to establish its outstanding balance.

Document whether an opening balance is start-of-day or end-of-day, the covered
history and any gaps. An end-of-day balance includes that date's movements: add
only subsequent movements. Reconcile imported historical funding and payments
against the starting position before including them. Unknown starting amounts
stay unknown. The utility's account cutover uses end-of-day openings explicitly;
adapt other conventions deliberately rather than silently shifting a date.

Separate the underlying purchase, the person who paid, the company obligation,
and the later repayment. A confirmed reimbursable purchase paid by a person does
not reduce company checking until the company actually pays. Retain receipt and
purchase/service dates where different, amount, currency, source reference,
payee, confirmation basis, and repayment status. An unknown payee stays unknown;
do not infer it from a receipt's customer name.

An authorized obligation register preserves original amount, cumulative
repayment, outstanding amount, and links to actual repayment transactions.
Partial repayment reduces only that obligation. Flag refunds, disputed amounts,
or overpayment for reconciliation rather than silently closing or rewriting it.
Record an actual company payment once, on its actual date and account, using
payment evidence. A prepared report or payment approval is not payment evidence.
Bookkeeping authority does not authorize initiating a transfer.

When accounting books already recognized a purchase and payable, repayment
settles the payable rather than recognizing the purchase twice. In a cash
tracker, preserve the actual outflow using the documented reporting method;
do not describe every reimbursement as an operating expense. Equipment,
inventory, prepayments, and other classifications may need separate treatment.
If classification remains unresolved, preserve the verified cash movement and
flag its treatment. Do not force an unsupported transaction type merely to fit
a dropdown or exclude a real movement from cash totals. Propose any needed
structure change separately within existing edit authority. Do not invent tax
deductibility, depreciation, business-use allocation, or a legal conclusion.

Keep capital contributions, revenue, reimbursements, distributions, loans, and
third-party collections distinct. Do not count the same cash through both an
opening balance and historical funding. An undated available balance is not a
dated posted balance. Commitments, draft invoices, issued invoices, and collected
cash are different states. Money held for another business is not automatically
the company's revenue. Label cash reports and obligation totals separately.

After authorized edits, freshly read back the affected record: original amounts
remain intact; repayments and outstanding balances reconcile; company cash
changes only when it actually moved; and purchases, funding, and repayments are
not duplicated. These checks establish record consistency, not tax acceptance
or professional approval.
