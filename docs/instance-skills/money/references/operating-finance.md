# Operating finance

Use the portions needed to maintain records, explain performance, or support a
business decision. This is not a mandatory accounting system or monthly ritual.
For recognition and reimbursement classification, also use bookkeeping-basis.md.

## Evidence and operating records

Recover the entity, account, currency, reporting basis, coverage period and as-of
date. A dated bank balance, processor balance, invoice register and progress report
answer different questions. Prefer direct evidence for the fact it establishes;
newer prose does not automatically supersede older transaction evidence. Describe
material conflicts, incomplete coverage, and reported versus directly verified
status. Request only evidence whose absence changes the entry or decision.

For an authorized register, preserve stable identifiers, counterparties, original
amounts, issue/service/purchase dates, due dates, currency, source links, payment
references, adjustments and outstanding amounts. Do not require every field for
every record; missing consequential facts stay visible. Distinguish proposed or
signed orders, draft and issued invoices, deposits, earned revenue, collections,
and bank settlement. Mark overdue amounts using the stated as-of date and terms.

Link partial payments, credits and refunds to the original item. Outstanding
amount equals the documented amount due less applicable payments and credits;
disputes and overpayments require explicit exceptions, not silent closure. An
invoice status or payment approval alone is not payment evidence. Preparing a
payment-readiness assessment does not send invoices, contact debtors, or pay bills.

## Bank and processor reconciliation

Preserve allocation relationships: one payment may cover several invoices and
several payments may cover one invoice. Check allocations against both payment
and invoice totals; show unapplied cash, credits and overpayments. A refund after
settlement links back to the original collection/allocation while its new cash
movement retains its own date. Settlement matching can require many-to-many
relationships; fuzzy similarity proposes candidates, never confirmed linkage.

Match records using transaction/settlement IDs, account, currency, dates and amounts.
One settlement can contain multiple collections, fees, refunds, reserves or other
adjustments; aggregate matching does not justify erasing component evidence.
Explain gross-to-net differences and timing before proposing corrections.

Maintain distinct bank and processor movements. A USD 300 collection with a USD 9
fee and USD 291 bank settlement contributes 300 gross collections and 9 fees under
a cash operating report, but only 291 to checking. Do not count collection and
settlement twice in consolidated cash. Transfers between owned accounts cancel
in consolidated cash; each account still reconciles separately. Pending balances,
withheld reserves, chargebacks and third-party funds are not freely available cash.

Reconcile opening balance plus unique movements to closing balance for each
account and coverage period. Record unmatched, missing, duplicated or disputed
items and their decision implications. Do not invent fees or balancing entries
to force agreement, or mark a reconciliation complete without sufficient evidence.

## Cash forecast and performance

For payment readiness, maintain payment acceptance, bank verification and
settlement readiness as separate dated states. Preserve reported, directly
verified and unresolved evidence. Activation or bank linking proves neither
available cash nor completed settlement. Identify outstanding contact, receipt,
address-visibility, fee and order-term reviews when relevant to customer use.
Record actual fee terms and selected services in the owning records, not in the
portable skill. This assessment grants no account changes or invoice sending.

Start a forecast from a dated verified balance with explicit coverage. Show
expected receipt/payment dates, confidence or conditional status, amounts owed,
restricted funds and already committed outflows. Separate actual, budget and
forecast; approval to spend and historical opening funding are not current cash.
Avoid double subtraction of obligations already included in forecast payments.

Use an appropriate weekly or monthly horizon, not a fixed universal schedule.
Show potential shortfalls and test decisive assumptions such as late collections,
refunds or lower sales. Available cash, operating result and profit are different
outputs. Runway requires a defensible burn basis; seasonal or irregular flows may
need a dated forecast rather than one average ratio. Do not invent reserves or
minimum balances: use approved constraints or clearly labeled proposals.

Compare actuals and budget using the same entity, basis, categories, currency and
period coverage. Show amount variance and percentage only with a meaningful
denominator. Expense increases and revenue increases have different favorable
directions; zero budgets do not yield a meaningful percent variance. Explain the
few material drivers supported by evidence, including timing, price, volume or
scope where observable. Preserve original budgets and assumptions beside revisions.
Connect the result to the decision, available choices, and applicable human owner.

For substantive operating reviews, use the existing record/report to show dated
available cash, amounts owed and due, potential shortfalls, material exceptions
and the next human decision. Do not require a dashboard or a full close for a
narrow task. Keep source evidence, extracted facts and calculations distinguishable;
source origin alone does not establish complete coverage or correct treatment.

## Product businesses when relevant

Distinguish inventory acquisition, units sold, cost recognition and payment dates.
Recover the documented cost basis, quantities, returns and stock adjustments;
do not treat all inventory purchases as cost of goods sold or invent valuation.
Customer deposits may carry delivery/refund obligations. Held third-party funds
and related remittances must remain separate from the company's own revenue.
Reconcile quantity and cash evidence where supplied. Missing accounting treatment
does not justify losing a verified cash movement or manufacturing margin.

Use opportunity-assessment.md for pricing, demand, delivery capacity, acquisition
and full cost assumptions. Feed observed collections, costs, refunds and effort
back into scenarios while preserving the original estimate and its limitations.
