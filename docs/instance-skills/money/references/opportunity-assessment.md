# Business opportunity assessment

Identify the decision, the person who decides, the purpose served, permitted
inputs, and existing financial or effort limits. Do not turn a budget question
into a new business or replace the chosen purpose with a marketable idea.
Affordability alone does not establish usefulness; compare purpose, uncertainty,
reversibility, maintenance burden, and effects on existing obligations.

Distinguish supplied facts, observed results, estimates, scenarios, and approved
thresholds. Suggested prices, quotas, earnings floors, response times, or service
commitments remain proposals. Ask only for missing human judgment that could
change the recommendation; continue independent supported calculations.

## Evidence that changes the decision

Research is useful when a plausible finding would change a named decision and
is worth its cost. Bound the question, markets, dates, required evidence, and
authorized effort. Use current authoritative sources for changing facts or
consequential financial conclusions, and preserve source dates and limits.
Reading or drafting a research assignment does not dispatch it or authorize
sharing private records with an external service.

For commercial offers, compare buyer problems or purchasing evidence with
supplier pricing and delivery evidence where relevant. Name differences in
buyer, geography, scope, service level, and period. Advertised prices and stated
budgets are not verified transactions or proof of demand for a different offer.
Missing evidence is not adverse evidence; a bounded search is not a universal
claim. Review returned research against its sources, arithmetic, requested
deliverables, and reasoning. Keep provider claims separate from inspected
evidence and your inference. Missing support limits only dependent conclusions.

## Economics without false precision

- Separate proposed sales, earned revenue, and collected cash. State periods and
  currencies; do not aggregate currencies without a supported conversion.
- Define cash contribution as revenue less identified variable cash costs,
  including applicable fees and acquisition. It is not profit or owner take-home.
  A profit claim states included and excluded labor, overhead, taxes, and costs.
- Include acquisition, delivery, review, revisions, and support in effort hours.
  Do not invent a wage or count hypothetical time saved as cash income.
- Capacity limits delivery; demand needs separate support. Unknown demand yields
  conditional scenarios, not expected sales. Include payment delays, deposits,
  refunds, fees, and committed outflows when liquidity affects the decision.
- Count unsuccessful acquisition attempts once. With zero wins, per-win cost is
  undefined; retain period cash and effort. With zero hours, do not report an
  infinite hourly return. Any contribution-per-hour ratio is a scenario ratio,
  not a wage or realized return.
- For positive unit contribution, fixed-cost break-even is fixed costs divided
  by contribution, rounded up for indivisible units. State the period, omitted
  costs, capacity, and required demand. Nonpositive contribution cannot cover
  positive fixed costs through more volume under unchanged assumptions.

Vary the assumptions that could change the decision, preserving original
estimates beside observations and revisions. Do not invent universal profit
thresholds or combine all favorable assumptions into an unlabeled forecast.

Recommend one reasoned disposition: proceed within existing authority, propose
a bounded experiment, defer, or decline. Identify the decisive uncertainty and
tradeoff. An experiment states its question, observation, cash/effort ceilings,
and stopping conditions, including necessary preparation and follow-up. One sale
is evidence of that purchase, not repeatable acquisition or business viability.
Further investigation needs a decision-relevant reason, not a desire for agreement.
Financial judgment never supplies missing spending or commitment authority.
